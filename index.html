Why switching wins twice as often

Your first pick is right 1 / 3 of the time, so the car is behind one of the other two doors 2 / 3 of the time. The host never opens the car door and never opens yours — so when they open a goat, that whole 2 / 3 collapses onto the single door left standing. Staying wins with your original 1 / 3; switching wins with the other 2 / 3.

The host's knowledge is what does the work. If they opened a door at random and it happened to show a goat, both remaining doors would sit at 1 / 2 and switching would gain you nothing.

Problem posed by Steve Selvin (1975), popularised by Marilyn vos Savant (1990) — background on Wikipedia.
